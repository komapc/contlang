## Task (pass 2 of 3: mark the words)

Each numbered line is a plain English sentence. It will be translated into min-co (spec below, for reference only — do NOT
write min-co now). Mark which pieces will become ONE min-co word each, by wrapping each piece in { }. A min-co word has at most
3 roots from the list of 45, a part of speech, and grammar marks (tense, number, negation, comparison, intensity, definiteness,
passive voice, command, question), so small grammar words go INSIDE the piece they modify: {the dogs}, {did not see},
{will go}, {very cold}, {more easily}.
- One piece for one concept: a phrasal verb ({give up}), a fixed compound ({parking lot}), a word with its degree word
  ({very cold}), a verb with its auxiliaries and "not".
- Separate pieces for a free combination where each word adds its own meaning ({old} {house}, {run} {fast}).
- Split a word into 2 or 3 pieces when its meaning cannot be built from 3 basic roots but its parts can
  ("ballplayer" → {person} {who plays} {ball game}); do it only when it helps a reader.
- Prepositions of place, time and cause, and words like "because", are pieces of their own ({behind}, {because}).
- Only "and", "if/when/although" at the start of a clause, and clause boundaries stay outside braces.
Do not change the words of the sentence except for such splits.

Reply in exactly this format, one line per input line, nothing else:
1. {piece} {piece} and {piece} ...
2. ...
