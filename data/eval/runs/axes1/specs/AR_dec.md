# min-co: how a word is written

A word is `ROOTS | FORM`.

- ROOTS: a noun, adjective or adverb has exactly ONE root (with a value if useful). A verb has 1 to 3 roots: the action
  first, then what narrows it — the body part (`BODY(=v)`), the manner, or the kind of thing acted on.
  If one root is not enough for a noun, adjective or adverb, add a role (see Form marks) or write a phrase (see Phrases).
- FORM: the part of speech, then optional marks.
  - Parts of speech: `o` noun, `i` verb, `a` adjective, `e` adverb.
  - Example: `PLACE(=0) | o` = building; `{ PLACE(=0) | o } FOR { LIVE | i }` = house.

A root may carry a value on its axis, `ROOT(=v)`, with v from -5 to +5.
The anchors listed with each root name some points of its axis. **Every value from -5 to +5 is usable**: a value between
two anchors means something between them (`GOOD(=+1)` = okay, `GOOD(=+4)` = very good, `BIG(=-3)` = quite small).
Pick the value that fits best; do not round to ±5. With no value, the root is meant plainly (`GOOD` = good).
HAPPEN never takes a value.

## Form marks

Marks go after the part of speech. They are not roots and do not count toward the limit of 3.

Noun roles turn the meaning into a related noun:
- `agent`: the one who does it, or a person of that field (person or thing). `ART(=+5) | o agent` = artist. `TEXT | o agent` = writer, `HAPPEN | o agent` = cause.
- `tool`: the means or instrument. `TEXT | o tool` = pen, `JOIN(=-4) | o tool` = knife.
- `result`: what comes out of it. `HAPPEN | o result` = consequence, `TEXT | o result` = a text.
- `quality`: the quality or state itself. `GOOD | o quality` = goodness, `CAN | o quality` = ability.

Grammar marks, each with a value from -5 to +5; not written means unspecified:
- T: tense (-2 past, +2 future).
- R: event before (-3) or after (+3) the reference time.
- A: aspect (-5 beginning … +5 completed).
- N: number (+1 few, +3 plural, +5 all).
- M: negation and probability (-5 not, -3 maybe, +4 certainly).
- C: comparison (-3 less, +3 more, +5 most).
- I: intensity (-5 barely … +4 very, +5 utterly).
- D: definiteness (-3 a, +3 the).
- V: voice (-4 passive, +4 deliberate).
- K: make someone do it (-5 prevent … 0 let … +5 force).
- `!`: command.
- `?`: question.

Write the letter and the value together, without `(=`: `MOVE | i T-2` = moved, `SOMEONE | o N+3 D+3` = the people.

## Phrases

A concept may be written as ONE word, or as a phrase of up to 3 words: `{ X } LINK { Y }` or `{ X } LINK { Y } LINK { Z }`.
X is the head and carries the part of speech of the whole concept; each next word narrows the one before it. Every word
has its own braces and its own FORM. LINK is the first of these that fits:
- `FROM`: X is made of Y, or comes from Y.
- `FOR`: X is made for Y, used to do Y.
- `AGAINST`: X protects from Y, stops or fights Y.
- `LIKE`: X looks like Y, or is done like Y.
- `PI`: X is part of Y, or is found in Y.

## Quotes

Only proper names, digits and scientific names of particular species or substances are written in quotes: `"Rome"`, `"17"`, `"Canis"`, `"iodine"`.
Everything else is built from roots.

## Sentences

A sentence is a sequence of words and particles. Every word has its own `| FORM`; a word is written inside its own braces { }.
- `E`: the next word is the direct object of the verb before it.
- `LA [ … ]`: a context or condition clause before the main clause (if, when, although).
- `[ … ]`: a clause used inside another, as its object or modifier.
- `;`: separates independent clauses.
- `AND`: joins two words of the same kind.
- A word with part of speech `i` is the verb; there is no separate predicate particle. An adjective or noun used as the predicate also takes `i`: `PLACE(=0) | o BIG | i` = the building is big.
CLAUSES: `;` separates independent clauses of a long sentence; `[ ... ]` marks a nested or argument clause. Every word has its own `| FORM`.

WORD BOUNDARIES: every word is written inside its own braces `{ ROOTS | FORM }`; particles, `[ ]` and `;` stand outside braces.
