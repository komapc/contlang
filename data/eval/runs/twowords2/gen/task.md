# Task: describe each word by two other words

For each numbered English noun in words.md (after the dash: its main sense; describe the noun in that sense), write a pair "X LINK Y" such that a reader who sees only X, LINK and Y would guess the noun.

- X is the kind: a broad, common English noun naming what sort of thing it is (person, place, tool, event, animal, liquid, feeling, building, money, ...).
- Y is one common English noun that narrows X down to the target.
- LINK is the first of these that fits:
  - FROM: X is made of Y (statue FROM bronze).
  - FOR: X is made for, used for, or exists for Y (knife = tool FOR meat; bed = furniture FOR sleep).
  - LIKE: X looks like or behaves like Y.
  - PI: X is part of Y, or is found in Y, or belongs to Y (wheel = part PI car; teacher = person PI school).
- X and Y must each be one single common word, neither may be the target word itself or share its stem (not "piano" for pianist).
- Choose the pair a reader would decode most surely, not the most precise definition.

Write answers.md with exactly one line per noun, same numbering:
N. X | LINK | Y
