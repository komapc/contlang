The philosophy of min-co

min-co (minimal + continuous) is a minimal philosophical language (artlang) for precise and flexible thought.

  • A minimum of grammar and vocabulary (inspired by Toki Pona and Esperanto).
  • A maximum of expressiveness through a small set of universal continuous features.
  • Concepts are not categories but positions in a conceptual space.
  • Nuance instead of a label: the meaning of a word is a root plus continuous adjustments.

Principles

  1. A small set of general roots. About 45 general concepts not tied to a part of speech (move, think, want, good, big, …), mostly NSM primitives; most have their own axis. The list and the rules for adding to it are in roots.yaml and the lexicon.
  2. The part of speech works like Esperanto suffixes, in the form after the |. o noun, i verb, a attribute, e adverbial; a noun may have the role agent, tool, result, quality (-ist, -il, -aĵ, -ec). Any root takes any part of speech: MOVE | o (movement), MOVE | i (to move), MOVE | a (mobile), MOVE | e (movingly). The form is separate from the meaning: a root does not know what part of speech it is.
  3. Axes are the only continuous mechanism. The semantic adjustments of a word are axes with a value from -5 to +5 and a scope: each root has its own axis or none (GOOD, BIG, TIME, …); there are no shared unnamed axes. There are no separate “gradient roots” or local axes: they are the same axes (model).
  4. Features are optional. An axis that is not written has an unspecified value (the zero rule: it costs nothing in bits); (=0) is the explicit middle. The marker ? requests a value (see syntax, model).
  5. Minimal grammar. The head first, the particles E, PI, LA, the link words FROM, FOR, LIKE, brackets and ?; the predicate is the word with | i, without LI (syntax).
  6. A not-quite-infinite vocabulary. Words are generated as roots + axis values + form; compounds (two words joined by a link word) are built by the same logic. Stable recipes are fixed by testing, but there is no need to memorize a vocabulary.
  7. The goal is the semantic field. A code has succeeded if the reader names a word from the same field (house, dwelling, hut), not necessarily the same word.
  8. Math does the encoding. The code of a word on the site is built by math (word and root vectors), not by a language model; the model serves only as reader and judge in blind tests (math). The model of simplicity is Toki Pona.

Where this came from

The scheme was not invented entirely by hand but selected through experiments on data (English, ConceptNet Numberbatch embeddings, 3000 words): global axes, roots with local axes, roots with universal axes, and a continuous form against a suffix were compared. Details and metrics: work plan (historical, in Russian), the data/ directory.
